MBALE • COMMUNITY

Mbale police officers urged to strengthen open dialogue and discipline

September 7, 2026 • Mbale, Uganda

Police leadership in Mbale has encouraged officers to use open and constructive dialogue to raise concerns affecting their welfare and work.

Commissioner of Police Paul Nkore held a Baraza with officers at Mbale Police Barracks on September 7, giving personnel an opportunity to openly discuss challenges affecting them and explore possible solutions.

According to the Uganda Police Force, officers were encouraged to speak freely about concerns arising in the course of their duties and within the barracks. CP Nkore listened to the issues raised and emphasized that constructive dialogue can help identify practical solutions.

The engagement forms part of community-policing and internal engagement efforts aimed at improving communication, discipline and working relationships. Police leadership said meaningful solutions are easier to find when personnel can raise concerns through appropriate channels.

For communities in Mbale, effective policing depends not only on enforcement but also on public confidence, professional conduct and communication between officers and the people they serve.

BUSINESS • MBALE

Mushroom growing gives Mbale women a new route to income

September 8, 2026 • Mbale, Uganda

Women in Mbale are turning mushroom cultivation into a commercial activity, using controlled growing conditions to produce edible mushrooms and create income.

Mushroom production is changing the way some farmers approach a crop that was once mainly collected from natural environments. Instead of waiting for wild mushrooms to appear seasonally, growers can create controlled conditions that allow selected edible varieties to be produced more consistently.

The move is opening another livelihood opportunity for women in Mbale, particularly for farmers looking for enterprises that can be undertaken on relatively small spaces. The crop can be processed and sold fresh or as sun-dried mushrooms, creating opportunities beyond primary production.

The growth of mushroom enterprises also highlights the wider role of agricultural diversification in urban and peri-urban communities. For households facing pressure on traditional farming activities, smaller high-value enterprises can provide additional sources of income when production and markets are managed well.

For Mbale and the wider Bugisu region, such enterprises add to the area's growing interest in agricultural value addition, entrepreneurship and food-based businesses.

EASTERN UGANDA • AGRICULTURE

Eastern Uganda seeks to revive cotton farming as production declines

September 3, 2026 • Eastern Uganda

Stakeholders in Eastern Uganda are pushing to revive cotton production after years of decline in an area where the crop was once a major source of household income.

Cotton was historically known as Eastern Uganda's “white gold”, supporting farmers and businesses across districts including Mbale, Bukedea, Pallisa and Kumi. However, the crop has lost ground as farmers increasingly choose food crops and other cash crops.

The Uganda Cotton Development Organisation has reported that cotton production in Eastern Uganda has declined by about 40 percent over the past decade. The decline has raised concern among stakeholders who see renewed cotton production as a potential source of income and economic activity.

Efforts to reverse the trend include mobilising farmers and rebuilding interest in cotton production. Afric Co-operative Society Ltd is among the organisations involved in efforts to encourage farmers to return to the crop.

A successful revival would depend on reliable markets, good agronomic practices, quality planting material, farmer organisation and returns that make cotton competitive with alternative crops. For farmers, the decision to plant cotton ultimately depends on whether the crop can provide a dependable livelihood.

HEALTH • BUKEDI

Bukedi communities see progress in the fight against malaria

September 9, 2026 • Eastern Uganda

Health workers and communities in Bukedi are reporting encouraging signs in malaria prevention and treatment, although the disease remains a significant public-health concern.

Malaria has historically placed a heavy burden on households and health facilities across Bukedi. The disease can keep children away from school and adults away from work, while repeated illness places pressure on family finances and health services.

Recent efforts are increasingly emphasizing prevention at household level, early diagnosis and prompt treatment. Health workers and communities are using these approaches to reduce the impact of malaria and prevent severe illness.

The improvement does not mean the problem has disappeared. Continued prevention, access to testing and treatment, and community participation remain important if the gains are to be sustained.

The experience in Bukedi demonstrates how public-health progress can depend on everyday household practices as much as on health-facility services.

UGANDA • CLIMATE & AGRICULTURE

Dry conditions put pressure on farmers and families across Uganda

September 9, 2026 • Uganda

Prolonged dry conditions are affecting water sources, crops and livestock in several parts of Uganda, including communities in the north-east and Bugisu.

Areas that normally benefit from seasonal rainfall have experienced depleted water sources and stressed crops. Communities in parts of Karamoja, Teso, Greater Masaka, Bugisu and West Nile are among those experiencing the effects.

For farmers, dry weather can reduce crop performance and increase pressure on livestock as pasture and water become harder to access. Families may also face higher costs when food production falls and water becomes less available.

The situation shows why climate information, water conservation and resilient farming practices are increasingly important for communities whose livelihoods depend heavily on agriculture.

Uganda's official seasonal outlook identifies September to December as a major rainfall season for much of the country, but local conditions can vary considerably. Farmers therefore need timely weather information alongside practical measures for managing climate risks.
