NATIONAL • TOORO KINGDOM

Tooro succession dispute deepens after King Oyo's death

September 10, 2026 • National Desk
Photo: public image shared by Queen Kemigisa's X account. Used here as a historical reference image; this is not a photo of the current succession announcement.

The Tooro Kingdom is facing a major succession dispute after the death of King Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV, with the royal clan's choice of Prince Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma being challenged by members of the late king's family.

King Oyo died on August 27, 2026, at the age of 34 while receiving cancer treatment in the United States. He had become the world's youngest reigning monarch when he was crowned at the age of three in 1995.

The royal succession committee announced Prince Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma, a cousin of the late king and a news anchor and editor at Uganda Broadcasting Corporation, as the new monarch.

Members of King Oyo's family have challenged that decision. Princess Ruth Komuntale said the late king's will provided for a son, if lawfully recognised as his biological son, to become heir. Queen Best Kemigisa also rejected the selection and called for the succession process to respect the late king's wishes.

The dispute has brought attention to the relationship between the late king's will, Tooro customs and the institutions responsible for determining succession. Kingdom leaders have called for calm and a structured process.

Tooro remains a culturally influential traditional kingdom within Uganda. Its monarch does not exercise sovereign political power, but the institution has an important social and cultural role for its people.

MBALE • AGRICULTURE • WOMEN

Mushroom growing gives Mbale women a new route to income

September 8, 2026 • Mbale, Uganda
Illustrative photo: mushroom cultivation training in Eastern Uganda, The Boon Project. The photograph is not presented as the exact Mbale group pictured in the report.

Women in Mbale are turning mushroom cultivation into a commercial activity, using controlled growing conditions to produce edible mushrooms and create income.

The Mushroom Growers in Mbale group, led by Annet Shakira Mutende, has used the enterprise as an alternative livelihood. The group began mushroom growing in 2011 and members say the activity can help with food, school fees and other household needs.

Mushrooms can be grown on relatively small spaces, making the enterprise attractive to farmers who do not have large areas of land. The women market fresh and dried mushrooms to households and other buyers.

However, growers face challenges including input costs, access to quality spawn, inconsistent prices, storage limitations and the need for reliable markets. Expanding production therefore requires not only farming skills but also stronger marketing and value addition.

The Mbale experience shows how small-scale agricultural enterprises can create additional income while contributing to food security.

Source: Daily Monitor, Phoebe Masongole, September 8, 2026. Photo above is clearly labelled illustrative and is credited to The Boon Project.
EASTERN UGANDA • AGRICULTURE

Eastern Uganda seeks to revive cotton farming as production declines

September 3, 2026 • Eastern Uganda
Illustrative Uganda cotton photo: African Wildlife Foundation. The image is from a cotton project in Kidepo and is not claimed to be an Eastern Uganda farm.

Stakeholders in Eastern Uganda are pushing to revive cotton production after years of decline in an area where the crop was once a major source of household income.

Cotton was historically known as Eastern Uganda's “white gold”, supporting farmers and businesses across districts including Mbale, Bukedea, Pallisa and Kumi. The crop has lost ground as farmers have shifted toward food and alternative cash crops.

The Uganda Cotton Development Organisation has reported that cotton production in Eastern Uganda declined by about 40 percent over the past decade.

Revival efforts include farmer mobilisation and rebuilding interest in cotton production. Afric Co-operative Society Ltd is among organisations involved in encouraging farmers to return to the crop.

A sustainable revival will depend on reliable markets, quality planting material, good agronomic practices, farmer organisation and returns that make cotton competitive with other crops.

Source: Daily Monitor, Phoebe Masongole and Fred Wambede, September 3, 2026. Cotton photo credited to African Wildlife Foundation and labelled illustrative.
HEALTH • BUKEDI

Bukedi communities see progress in the fight against malaria

September 9, 2026 • Eastern Uganda
Illustrative malaria-prevention photo: Panos Pictures / Sven Torfinn. It illustrates community malaria education and is not claimed to be the exact Kadakolene Health Centre training.

Health workers and communities in Bukedi are reporting encouraging signs in malaria prevention and treatment, although the disease remains a significant public-health concern.

Malaria has historically placed a heavy burden on households and health facilities across Bukedi. Illness can keep children away from school and adults away from work while repeated infections increase pressure on household finances.

Recent efforts have increasingly emphasized prevention at household level, early diagnosis and prompt treatment. These measures can reduce severe illness and help communities maintain progress.

The improvement does not mean malaria has disappeared. Continued prevention, access to testing and treatment, and community participation remain important if gains are to be sustained.

Source: Daily Monitor, Mudangha Kolyangha, September 9, 2026. Photo is credited to Panos Pictures and labelled illustrative.
UGANDA • CLIMATE & AGRICULTURE

Dry conditions put pressure on farmers and families across Uganda

September 9–10, 2026 • Uganda
Illustrative drought photo from Uganda. It is used to show the type of agricultural stress described in the report and is not claimed to be the exact field in the current story.

Prolonged dry conditions are affecting water sources, crops and livestock in several parts of Uganda, with communities in areas including the north-east, Bugisu and West Nile facing pressure.

For farmers, dry weather can reduce crop performance and increase pressure on livestock as pasture and water become harder to access. Families may also face higher costs when food production falls.

Uganda's September–December 2026 seasonal climate outlook says many areas are expected to transition from intermittent or isolated rains to more consistent rainfall later in September, with peak rainfall in many eastern areas around late October to November.

For the Mount Elgon region, including Mbale, Sironko, Bulambuli, Manafwa, Bududa, Namisindwa, Kapchorwa, Kween and Bukwo, official guidance expects intermittent rains through mid-to-late September followed by more consistent rainfall, with a peak around mid-October to mid-November.

Farmers can use climate information to plan planting, conserve soil and water, diversify crops and improve post-harvest handling.

Sources: Daily Monitor and Uganda Media Centre seasonal climate outlook. Photo is labelled illustrative.

EASTERN UGANDA • COFFEE • AGRICULTURE

Coffee seedlings dry up in Katakwi as farmers question planting timing

September 7, 2026 • Katakwi, Teso

Farmers in Katakwi are reporting losses after thousands of government-distributed coffee seedlings dried up during a prolonged dry spell.

The affected programme forms part of a wider push to expand commercial agriculture in Teso. Farmers say the timing of distribution during dry conditions made it difficult to keep seedlings alive and are calling for better planning, water access and farmer training.

The report says the government had launched a campaign to distribute more than 80 million high-value coffee seedlings across the Teso sub-region, with the aim of moving more farmers toward commercial crops.

The episode highlights the importance of matching seedling distribution with seasonal rainfall, water availability and practical extension support. For coffee growers, survival during the establishment stage is critical before a plantation can become productive.

Source: Business Focus, September 7, 2026. Mbale Media Daily summary.
EASTERN UGANDA • BUSINESS • LOCAL GOVERNMENT

Bukedea traders demand clearer cattle-market charges

September 1, 2026 • Bukedea, Eastern Uganda

Traders at Bukedea's weekly cattle market are asking the Town Council to review and clearly publish market charges as the council searches for a new contractor.

The council took over management of the market after the previous contractor, Orongat Contractors and Vendors Association, failed to remit more than Shs216 million in accumulated government revenue.

Traders have complained about charges they say were excessive under the former operator. They argue that high costs reduced the number of vendors and buyers who traditionally travelled to Bukedea from across Uganda and neighboring countries.

A transparent schedule of approved charges could help restore confidence among traders while the council reorganizes management of the market.

Source: Uganda Radio Network, September 1, 2026. Mbale Media Daily summary.
