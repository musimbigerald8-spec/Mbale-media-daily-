King Oyo buried in Fort Portal as Tooro bids farewell

Tooro Kingdom has bid farewell to Omukama Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV in Fort Portal, closing a nine-day mourning period and beginning a new chapter for the kingdom.

September 12, 2026 • Mbale Media Daily National Desk • By Mbale Media Daily

Tooro Kingdom mourning ceremony. Photo: Uganda Media Centre.

Thousands of mourners, cultural leaders, government officials and regional delegations gathered in Fort Portal on Saturday as Tooro Kingdom held the final ceremonies for King Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV.

Reuters reported that royal guards led the hearse carrying the king's casket through Fort Portal before a ceremony at St John's Cathedral. The casket was draped in the blue-and-yellow Tooro flag before the burial at the Karambi Royal Tombs.

A historic farewell

Oyo became Omukama in 1995 at the age of three and remained one of Uganda's most recognisable traditional leaders for more than three decades. He died at the age of 34 after receiving treatment for cancer in the United States.

The burial on September 12 carries added significance because the date is also associated with his 1995 coronation. His final send-off brought together mourners from Tooro and other parts of Uganda as well as dignitaries and cultural representatives.

The succession dispute

The farewell has taken place alongside a dispute over the succession to the Tooro throne. A royal succession committee selected Oyo's cousin, Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma, as the new king. Oyo's immediate family has challenged that decision and has referred to a will that they say identifies a biological son as an heir.

The competing claims have generated national attention. The reported existence and eligibility of the alleged heir remain disputed, and the succession process is separate from the completed burial of the late king.

Oyo's legacy

Oyo's reign was closely associated with the preservation of Tooro's cultural identity and with work involving young people, health, tourism and environmental conservation. His death has been widely described as a significant loss for Batooro and for Uganda's cultural heritage.

With the burial now completed, attention will turn to the future leadership and unity of the Tooro Kingdom. The succession question is likely to remain a major national story as the royal family and clan leadership work through the competing claims.
