National • Tooro Kingdom

Tooro succession dispute deepens after King Oyo's death

The Tooro Kingdom is facing a major succession dispute following the death of King Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV on August 27, 2026. The former boy king died at the age of 34 in the United States while receiving cancer treatment. His death has opened a sensitive conversation about who should lead the kingdom and how the succession should be handled.

The royal succession committee announced Prince Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma, a cousin of the late king and a news anchor and editor with Uganda Broadcasting Corporation, as the new monarch. The announcement came as preparations continued for King Oyo's burial in Fort Portal.

However, members of King Oyo's family have challenged the decision. Princess Ruth Komuntale said the late king's will provided for a son, if lawfully recognized as his biological child, to become heir. Queen Best Kemigisa also objected to the selection and called for the succession process to respect the late king's wishes.

The dispute has raised questions about royal customs, the contents of the will and the institutions responsible for determining succession. The kingdom's leadership has called for calm and a structured process rather than competing announcements.

The story remains significant beyond the royal family because Tooro is one of Uganda's traditional kingdoms and its monarch has an important cultural and social role even though traditional kingdoms do not exercise sovereign political power.

Mbale • Agriculture • Women

Mushroom farming gives Mbale women a new route to income

Mushroom cultivation is becoming an income-generating activity for women in Mbale City. Unlike wild mushrooms, which traditionally appear seasonally, cultivated mushrooms can be produced under controlled conditions, giving farmers an opportunity to plan production and sales.

The Mushroom Growers in Mbale group, led by Annet Shakira Mutende, has built its enterprise around the idea that mushroom farming can help women respond to household financial pressures. The activity can contribute to food supply while also creating an income stream for school fees, household needs and other expenses.

The development also shows how farmers can diversify beyond traditional crops. With suitable skills, clean production conditions, reliable markets and proper post-harvest handling, mushrooms can become a small-business opportunity in urban and peri-urban communities.

For women seeking alternative livelihoods, the experience in Mbale provides an example of how agricultural enterprises can combine food production with income generation.

Eastern Uganda • Agriculture

Eastern Uganda seeks to revive cotton farming after years of decline

Cotton farming, once an important source of income for many households in eastern Uganda, has experienced a major decline. Farmers have increasingly shifted toward food crops and other commercial activities, while challenges affecting production and profitability have reduced cotton's appeal.

Stakeholders are now looking for ways to rebuild the crop. Revival efforts are expected to depend on farmer mobilisation, access to quality seed and inputs, better extension services, reliable buyers and stronger farmer organisations.

The decline matters because cotton can provide both farm income and raw material for Uganda's textile and manufacturing value chain. A stronger cotton sector could create opportunities beyond the farm, including ginning, transport, processing and trade.

For eastern Uganda, the challenge will be to make cotton commercially attractive while ensuring farmers have practical support and predictable markets.

Health • Eastern Uganda

Bukedi communities report progress in the fight against malaria

Communities in Bukedi are seeing signs of progress in efforts to reduce malaria, with prevention, early diagnosis and prompt treatment playing an important role. The fight remains important because malaria continues to affect families, especially in areas where environmental conditions can support mosquito breeding.

Prevention measures can reduce exposure, while early testing helps health workers identify infections before they become severe. Prompt treatment is also important for reducing complications and protecting households from prolonged illness.

Community participation remains central. Health workers and residents need to continue supporting prevention measures, seeking testing when symptoms appear and following treatment instructions.

The experience in Bukedi shows that progress against malaria depends on consistent action rather than one-time campaigns.

Agriculture • Climate

Dry conditions put pressure on farmers and families across Uganda

Prolonged dry conditions have affected communities in several parts of Uganda, with depleted water sources, stressed livestock and crops struggling where rainfall has been insufficient. Reports have highlighted impacts from Karamoja and Teso to Bugisu, West Nile, Sembabule and Greater Masaka.

For farming households, unreliable rainfall can affect planting decisions, crop growth, livestock feeding and household food supplies. When water sources decline, families may also spend more time and money looking for water for domestic use and animals.

The national seasonal climate outlook provides an important additional context: the September–December rainfall season is expected to bring improving rainfall in many areas, although the timing and intensity will vary by region.

In the Mount Elgon region, which includes Mbale, Sironko, Bulambuli, Manafwa, Bududa, Namisindwa, Kapchorwa, Kween and Bukwo, official forecasts indicate intermittent rains through mid-to-late September, followed by more consistent rainfall and a peak around mid-October to mid-November.

Farmers can use the changing conditions to plan carefully, conserve water, protect soil and select practices suited to the expected rainfall pattern.
