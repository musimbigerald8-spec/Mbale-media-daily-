Eastern Uganda • Bududa

Bududa leaders arrested over alleged PDM funds diversion

Three Bududa local leaders have been arrested following allegations that Shs1 million in Parish Development Model funds belonging to a beneficiary was accessed using her national identification details and transferred to a phone number registered in one suspect's name.

The case has raised questions about the safeguards used when beneficiaries access government-supported funds. The reported allegation involves the use of a beneficiary's national ID and a subsequent transfer of money to a number linked to one of the suspects.

The arrests form part of an investigation into the alleged diversion. At this stage, the allegations have not been determined by a court, and the suspects are entitled to due process as investigators establish what happened to the money and who authorised or carried out the transaction.

Parish Development Model funds are intended to support household economic activity through organised parish-level structures. Any confirmed misuse can affect the intended beneficiaries and undermine confidence in the programme.

Eastern Uganda • Bulambuli

Bulambuli launches Shs200m rehabilitation of Bunambutye-Girigi Road

Bulambuli District leaders have launched a Shs200 million rehabilitation project for the Bunambutye-Girigi road, a route that has remained difficult to use because of its poor condition.

The project is intended to improve movement along the road and make it easier for residents to reach markets, services and other destinations. Poor road conditions can increase travel time, transport costs and the difficulty of moving goods, particularly during periods of heavy rain.

The launch puts attention on the implementation of the rehabilitation works and the condition of the road as work progresses. Residents and local leaders will be able to assess whether the project delivers the expected improvement in access and reliability.

For communities along the route, the road is more than an infrastructure project: it is part of the local transport network used by households, traders, farmers and service providers.

Mbale Media Daily will follow the project, including progress on the ground, the sections covered and any updates from Bulambuli District authorities.

Kumi • Security

Eight youths held over night attacks in Kumi Municipality

Eight youths have been arrested during a joint security operation in South Division of Kumi Municipality over allegations linked to night attacks on residents.

The operation targeted suspected criminal elements accused of waylaying people during the early hours. Local leaders say night attacks have become a concern for residents who need to move around the municipality after dark or before sunrise.

Robert Apedel Odea, the LCIII chairperson for South Division, described the night attacks as a growing concern. The arrests are expected to form part of investigations aimed at establishing who was involved and whether the suspects can be connected to specific incidents.

Security operations of this nature must still follow the normal investigative and legal process. An arrest is not a finding of guilt, and evidence presented during investigations and any subsequent court process will determine the outcome.

Business • Uganda

NSSF revenue rises 85% to Shs6.51 trillion

The National Social Security Fund reported revenue of Shs6.51 trillion for the financial year ended June 2026, representing an 85 percent increase according to the reported results.

The reported performance comes alongside assets under management of Shs32.8 trillion. The figures place attention on the scale of Uganda's social security savings and the fund's ability to grow the pool of assets managed on behalf of members.

NSSF has also continued to emphasise wider access to saving, including efforts aimed at bringing more Ugandans into formal social security arrangements. Growth in contributions and investment assets can affect the fund's capacity to meet members' long-term obligations, subject to the fund's investment performance and operating environment.

For employers and workers, the figures also underline the importance of regular contributions and accurate member records. Members ultimately depend on the system to preserve and grow their retirement savings over time.

Uganda • Economy

Local governments urged to boost revenue and invest in industrialisation

Government officials have urged local governments to strengthen domestic revenue mobilisation and support industrialisation as preparations begin for the 2027/2028 budget.

Minister of State for Finance Amos Lugoloobi said revenue-led fiscal consolidation would be among the strategic shifts guiding the next budget. The message was delivered during a regional consultative workshop in Lira.

Local governments rely on a combination of transfers and locally generated revenue to deliver services. Improving collection, accountability and the efficiency of local revenue systems can therefore affect the resources available for local priorities.

The industrialisation message also places emphasis on moving beyond collection towards productive investment. Districts and municipalities can support local economic activity through infrastructure, business services, skills development and coordination with private investors.

The coming budget process will show how these priorities are translated into allocations and implementation plans. Mbale Media Daily will track the proposals and their implications for Eastern Uganda.

Education • Uganda

Agriculture leads Uganda vocational assessment results

Results from Uganda's vocational assessment cycle show strong performance among National Diploma agriculture candidates, with 98.2 percent reported to have attained full competence.

The result highlights the role of practical training in preparing students for work in agriculture and related fields. Agriculture remains central to Uganda's economy, creating demand for people with technical knowledge in production, processing, agribusiness and related services.

Education officials have nevertheless continued to point to the challenge of attracting more young people into agriculture and other technical programmes. Strong assessment outcomes can help demonstrate the value of vocational pathways, but training must also remain connected to real employment and enterprise opportunities.

For institutions, the results provide an opportunity to examine what is working in practical instruction and where additional support may be needed. For students, they show that technical qualifications can provide a route into specialised areas of the economy.
