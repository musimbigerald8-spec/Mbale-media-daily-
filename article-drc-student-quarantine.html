Government orders mandatory 21-day quarantine for students travelling from DRC

Uganda has ordered a mandatory 21-day quarantine period for students travelling from the Democratic Republic of Congo as health authorities maintain precautionary monitoring.

September 13, 2026 • Mbale Media Daily Health Desk • Source: Daily Monitor • By Mbale Media Daily

The government has introduced a 21-day quarantine requirement for students travelling from the Democratic Republic of Congo. The measure is part of precautionary health monitoring as Uganda manages the risk of disease importation through cross-border movement.

The requirement means affected students are expected to remain under the prescribed monitoring period rather than immediately returning to normal school routines. The 21-day period is intended to give health authorities time to observe travellers and identify possible symptoms that could require medical attention.

Why the 21-day period?

Public-health monitoring periods are designed around the time in which symptoms of a suspected infectious disease may become apparent. A monitored period can therefore help health teams identify illness early and reduce the chance of unnoticed transmission.

What this means for students and families

Students travelling from DRC and their families should follow instructions issued by Uganda's health authorities. Families should not bypass official screening, monitoring or quarantine arrangements because doing so can make disease surveillance more difficult.

Schools and education administrators may also need to coordinate with health officials where affected learners are due to report for the new academic term. The practical arrangements for individual students should be confirmed through the responsible health authorities and the institutions concerned.

Cross-border health vigilance

Uganda shares a long border with the Democratic Republic of Congo, making cross-border movement an important part of public-health preparedness. Monitoring travellers is one of the tools authorities can use when responding to a health threat or suspected outbreak.

For readers in Eastern Uganda, where cross-border trade and movement are significant, the development is a reminder that travellers should follow health screening instructions and seek guidance from official health authorities whenever travel restrictions or monitoring measures are announced.
