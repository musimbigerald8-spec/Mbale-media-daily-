Lifesong for Orphans says 844 children are currently served in Uganda

September 11, 2026 • Uganda • Mbale Media Daily • By Mbale Media Daily

Lifesong for Orphans says its ministry in Uganda is currently serving 844 children, combining education, discipleship, safe homes, medical care and continued support as part of a broader effort to tackle generational poverty.

The figures appear on Lifesong for Orphans' current Uganda country page, which also lists 122 local ministry staff and 43 jobs created through sustainable business. The organization says it works with local leaders and partners to provide holistic, Gospel-centered care to orphaned and vulnerable children.

The projects behind the mission

Lifesong's Uganda work is built around several connected projects rather than one service. The organization identifies discipleship, education, safe homes and medical care as key areas. Its partners provide Christian-based education, meals for vulnerable children and protected boarding environments for girls at high risk of sexual abuse.

In Bukeka, the Suubi Medical Center is described by Lifesong as serving an average of about 16,000 patients a year. The wider programme also includes safe water, food support, vocational development and sustainable-business activity intended to strengthen families and communities.

Recent Lifesong updates also describe construction and expansion. The organization has reported progress on a new high school and other campus buildings, with students imagining science rooms, computer lessons, libraries and larger classrooms as part of their future.

The vision for Uganda

  • Help orphaned and vulnerable children grow up knowing they are loved and valued.
  • Provide food, clothing, shelter, medical care and safe living environments.
  • Give children a quality education and a foundation for adult life.
  • Provide discipleship, mentoring and continued support as young people transition into adulthood.
  • Strengthen families and communities through vocational training, sustainable business and local partnerships.

Education, safe homes and medical care

Lifesong says its education partners provide quality education based on Christian principles. Its safe-home programme gives vulnerable children a protected environment with supervision, guidance and counselling. Medical personnel provide ongoing care for children and staff, while the school clinic treats common illnesses and minor injuries.

Meet the Ugandan leaders behind Global Hands of Hope

Global Hands of Hope says its mission is to empathize, evangelize, educate and engage orphaned children in sustainable development to help end generational poverty. The organization says it works through education, healthcare, food security, child protection, vocational training and a local church.

Ronnie Nalera

Founder / President

Global Hands of Hope says Nalera experienced severe hardship as a child, later studied Mass Communication at Makerere University, worked with the Uganda Amnesty Commission, served as a pastor and completed postgraduate study in project planning and social sector management. He and his wife, Ritah, founded Global Hands of Hope in 2007.

Ritah Nalera

Operations Director

The organization says Nalera holds a B.A. in Mass Communications from Makerere University, previously worked with the United Nations on sustainable agriculture among rural farmers, and serves as Director of Sponsorship and Communication in Uganda. She is particularly focused on children and women.

Global Hands of Hope also lists Arthur Murungi as Programs Director. Its public team information does not provide enough detail for Mbale Media Daily to add further biographical claims about him.

A model built around long-term change

The work combines child protection, education, healthcare, discipleship and economic opportunities. Lifesong says its broader approach is intended not only to care for children today but also to help prevent the cycle of poverty by strengthening families, caregivers and communities.

For Mbale Media Daily readers, the story is a look at how a Uganda-based partnership is using schools, safe homes, healthcare, food security and skills development to create longer-term opportunities for vulnerable children.
