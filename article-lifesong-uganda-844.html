Lifesong for Orphans says 844 children are currently served in Uganda

September 11, 2026 • Uganda • Mbale Media Daily

Lifesong for Orphans says its ministry in Uganda is currently serving 844 children, combining education, discipleship, safe homes, medical care and continued support as part of a broader effort to tackle generational poverty.

Photo: Global Hands of Hope. Published on its public website as part of its Uganda ministry material.

The figures appear on Lifesong for Orphans' current Uganda country page, which also lists 122 local ministry staff and 43 jobs created through sustainable business. The organization says it works with local leaders and partners to provide holistic, Gospel-centered care to orphaned and vulnerable children.

According to Lifesong, its Uganda programme aims to ensure that children receive food, clothing, shelter and medical care, while also gaining access to quality education and support as they transition into adult life.

Education, safe homes and medical care

The ministry lists discipleship, education, safe homes and medical care among its key areas of work. Its education partners provide Christian-based schooling and meals for vulnerable children. The Safe House programme provides a protected boarding environment for girls considered at high risk of sexual abuse, with supervision, guidance and counselling.

Photo: Lifesong for Orphans — Uganda students reading.

Lifesong also says the Suubi Medical Center in Bukeka, through ministry partners, provides healthcare to an average of about 16,000 patients a year. Its Uganda work is closely connected with Global Hands of Hope, a Ugandan-founded Christian nonprofit working in Bukeka.

Meet the Ugandan leaders behind Global Hands of Hope

Global Hands of Hope says its mission is to empathize, evangelize, educate and engage orphaned children in sustainable development to help end generational poverty. The organization says it works through education, healthcare, food security, child protection, vocational training and a local church.

Ronnie Nalera

Founder / President

Global Hands of Hope says Nalera experienced severe hardship as a child, later studied Mass Communication at Makerere University, worked with the Uganda Amnesty Commission, served as a pastor and completed postgraduate study in project planning and social sector management. He and his wife, Ritah, founded Global Hands of Hope in 2007.

Ritah Nalera

Operations Director

The organization says Nalera holds a B.A. in Mass Communications from Makerere University, previously worked with the United Nations on sustainable agriculture among rural farmers, and serves as Director of Sponsorship and Communication in Uganda. She is particularly focused on children and women.

Global Hands of Hope also lists Arthur Murungi as Programs Director. The organization's public team page does not provide a detailed biography for him, so Mbale Media Daily is not adding unverified claims about his background or responsibilities beyond the title published by the organization.

A model built around long-term change

Global Hands of Hope says its work in Bukeka is designed to go beyond short-term relief. Its programmes include Suubi Christian School, a community church, a medical clinic, food security initiatives, vocational training and child protection. Lifesong's Uganda page also highlights sustainable business as part of the effort to reduce generational poverty.

Recent Lifesong updates show the programme continuing to focus on children's personal development. In an August 2026 Uganda update, the organization described the gradual transformation of a young girl who had arrived fearful and withdrawn but later became more confident through consistent care, education and faith-based support.

The work offers a look at how child protection, education, healthcare and economic opportunities can be combined to give vulnerable children a stronger path into adulthood.
