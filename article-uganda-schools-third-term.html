Uganda schools reopen today for third term

Schools following Uganda's national/public curriculum begin third term today, Monday September 14, 2026. The official school calendar sets the term at 82 days, with closing day scheduled for Friday December 4, 2026.

September 14, 2026 • Mbale Media Daily Education Desk • Primary source: Uganda Ministry of Education and Sports • By Mbale Media Daily

Schools across Uganda using the national curriculum are returning for the third and final term of the 2026 academic year. The reopening date is Monday, September 14, and the term is scheduled to run until December 4.

The Ministry of Education and Sports has maintained the official calendar after misinformation circulated about a possible change to the reopening date. The ministry's published 2026 calendar lists Monday September 14 as the start of Term III.

What parents and learners need to know

The reopening applies to pre-primary, primary and secondary schools that follow Uganda's national/public curriculum. Parents and guardians should therefore rely on communication from their schools and the official Ministry calendar for reporting arrangements, fees, requirements and any school-specific instructions.

Because individual schools may have different reporting procedures, families should check directly with their school before travelling where a school has issued specific arrival instructions. The national calendar itself, however, remains the key reference for the start and end dates.

How long will third term last?

The Ministry's 2026 calendar identifies Term III as an 82-day term beginning September 14 and ending December 4. This makes it the final scheduled school term of the 2026 academic year for institutions covered by the calendar.

Why the date matters

The start of a new term brings renewed activity to communities. Transport operators, food vendors, stationery shops, uniform suppliers and other small businesses around schools often experience increased demand as learners return.

For families in Mbale and Eastern Uganda, the reopening also marks the return of normal school-related movement across trading centres and neighbourhoods. Learners, teachers and parents are encouraged to plan their journeys early and follow their schools' instructions.
