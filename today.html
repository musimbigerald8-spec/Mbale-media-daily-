Monday's news in full: the stories Uganda and Mbale readers need to know

NATIONAL • EDUCATION

Uganda schools reopen for third term as parents and learners return to class

Uganda's pre-primary, primary and secondary schools begin the third term today, Monday September 14. The official 2026 school calendar sets the third term to run for 82 instructional days, ending on December 4.

The reopening puts learners, teachers and parents back into the normal school routine after the second-term break. Families are expected to confirm school requirements, transport arrangements and attendance plans so that learners can settle into lessons quickly.

For schools, the start of term is also a period for organising teaching programmes, attendance records and assessment plans. Parents who have questions about dates or school requirements should rely on communication from their schools and the Ministry of Education and Sports rather than unverified social-media messages.

NATIONAL • TOORO KINGDOM

King Oyo buried as Tooro begins a new chapter under Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma

King Oyo Nyimba Kabamba Iguru Rukidi IV was buried at the Karambi Royal Tombs in Fort Portal on Saturday after days of mourning. Oyo became internationally known after ascending the Tooro throne at the age of three in 1995 and remained a major cultural figure until his death at 34.

The funeral followed a succession process in which the Babiito royal clan selected his cousin, Edward Rukidi Kijanangoma, as successor. Kijanangoma, a broadcaster, was formally presented as the new king during the ceremonies.

The transition remains sensitive. Members of Oyo's family have disputed the succession decision and referred to an alleged 2022 will and a claimed biological son. The existence and succession status of the alleged heir have been contested, so the issue remains a matter of dispute rather than an established fact. Church and government leaders have urged calm and unity.

NATIONAL • JUSTICE

Judiciary deploys judicial officers to handle 2026 election petitions

Uganda's Judiciary is beginning hearings for election petitions arising from the 2026 elections. The cases will be handled across High Court circuits and the Civil Division in Kampala, putting the courts at the centre of the legal process for resolving disputed results.

Election petitions are important because they provide a legal route for candidates and other eligible parties to challenge results on grounds recognised by law. The court process is separate from political debate: claims must be considered on the evidence and legal arguments presented before the courts.

The hearings are scheduled to run from September 14 to November 13, 2026. Readers should distinguish between a petition being filed and a court actually finding that an election result was invalid.

Sources: UG Standard and Daily Monitor reports on the September 14 commencement of election-petition hearings.
SPORTS • CRICKET

Cricket Cranes maintain strong start in Kigali after three wins

Uganda's Cricket Cranes have made a strong start to the ILT20 Continental Cup in Kigali. They opened the campaign by beating Sierra Leone by 114 runs, then defeated Botswana by five wickets before adding a 64-run victory over Rwanda.

The Sierra Leone match produced Uganda's highest-ever Men's T20 International team total at the time: 250 for three. Charles Musemeza and Gerald Olipa provided major contributions with the bat, while later matches saw Joseph Baguma and Robinson Obuya play key roles against Botswana.

The run gives the defending champions early momentum, but the tournament is not finished. Uganda's next listed fixture is against Sierra Leone on September 15, followed by further matches involving Botswana, Rwanda and Kenya.

Sources: Daily Monitor, NBS Sport and ChampionSport reports, September 10–13, 2026.
SPORTS • BASKETBALL

City Oilers part ways with Andrew Tendo after more than a decade with the club

City Oilers have parted ways with head coach Andrew Tendo, ending a coaching association that lasted more than a decade. Tendo served as an assistant for nine seasons before becoming head coach in 2023.

The change follows a difficult competitive period for the club. According to Daily Monitor, the Oilers again lost to the Blazers in a seven-game series, 4–3, leaving them without the title for a second consecutive season.

The next stage will be about continuity: the club will need to determine its technical direction, support the playing group and prepare for the next competitive cycle. The coaching change does not erase Tendo's long contribution to the club.

Source: Daily Monitor, September 11, 2026.
MBALE • EASTERN UGANDA

Monday in Mbale: education, enterprise, agriculture and tourism remain central to the local economy

The reopening of schools gives Mbale's trading centres another boost at the start of the week. Learners, teachers and parents generate activity for transport operators, food vendors, shops, stationery businesses and other small enterprises.

Agriculture remains equally important across the wider Eastern Uganda economy. Coffee and other crops support household incomes, while farmers continue to face the practical challenges of production costs, weather, market access and post-harvest handling.

Mbale's tourism potential also connects local businesses to accommodation, food, transport, guiding, events and cultural services. For the city to capture more value, businesses need reliable service, good visibility, quality products and stronger links across the tourism and agricultural value chains.

BUSINESS • EAST AFRICA

Electric mobility partnership points to growing African market for cleaner transport

Electric mobility is becoming a larger part of the African transport conversation as manufacturers, mobility companies and cities look for alternatives to conventional fuel-powered motorcycles and vehicles.

In Uganda, the opportunity is especially relevant to motorcycle transport because boda-bodas are a major part of everyday mobility. The business case depends on more than the vehicle itself: charging infrastructure, battery support, financing, maintenance and reliable spare parts all influence whether electric mobility can scale.

A September 12 report on a Spiro-Yadea partnership highlighted plans to expand electric mobility across Africa. For Eastern Uganda, wider adoption could eventually create new opportunities for charging businesses, technicians and mobility services if infrastructure develops alongside demand.

Source: UG Bulletin, September 12, 2026, on the Spiro-Yadea partnership.
GLOBAL • TRADE & ENERGY

Red Sea security tensions continue to matter for shipping, fuel and prices

Security problems around the Red Sea can affect the global economy because the route connects major trading markets. When ships face greater security risks, operators may reroute, extend journeys or pay higher insurance and operating costs.

Higher freight and energy costs can eventually reach consumers through imported goods and transport prices. East African economies such as Uganda are exposed indirectly because many goods and fuel products depend on international supply chains before reaching local markets.

The important point for readers is that an international shipping disruption does not automatically mean every Ugandan price will rise immediately. The effect depends on the duration of disruption, oil prices, freight costs, exchange rates and how much of a product is imported.

AFRICA • REGIONAL AFFAIRS

Nigeria raises concern over renewed anti-migrant violence in South Africa

Nigeria has raised concern over renewed violence affecting foreign nationals in South Africa. The issue has wider regional significance because both countries are major African economies and important diplomatic and commercial partners.

The central concern is protection of people and prevention of retaliation. Violence against migrants can also damage trade, community relations and diplomatic ties between countries whose citizens live and work across borders.

The story also shows why careful reporting matters: claims about individual incidents should be checked against credible evidence, while broader political statements should be attributed to the officials or governments making them.
