MBALE • EASTERN UGANDA

Mbale businesses have an opportunity to turn renewed city activity into local growth

Mbale begins the new week with education, trade, tourism and agriculture continuing to shape the city's economic activity.

The return of schools and the continued movement of residents through the city's commercial areas create opportunities for transport operators, food businesses, shops, service providers and other small enterprises. For local businesses, the opportunity is not only to attract customers but also to improve service, reliability and visibility.

Tourism is another area with potential. Mbale sits near major attractions connected to Mount Elgon and the wider Bugisu region. Growth in visitor activity can benefit accommodation providers, restaurants, guides, transport operators, craftspeople and event businesses when the different parts of the local value chain work together.

Agriculture remains central to the wider Eastern Uganda economy, particularly coffee and other high-value crops. Better production practices, access to markets and value addition can help farmers capture more of the final value of their products.

For Mbale Media Daily, the focus is on telling these local stories while highlighting practical opportunities, challenges and voices from the community.

BUSINESS • UGANDA

Shilling slides beyond Shs3,900 as higher oil prices increase pressure on importers

The Uganda shilling has weakened beyond Shs3,900 to the US dollar as global oil prices increase demand for foreign currency from fuel importers.

The currency move comes as international crude prices rise amid renewed instability around key energy and shipping routes. For Uganda, which imports most of its petroleum products, higher oil prices can quickly affect the domestic economy.

Fuel importers need dollars to settle international purchases. When demand for dollars rises while the supply of foreign currency is under pressure, the shilling can weaken. Businesses importing machinery, food, electronics and other goods may then face higher local-currency costs.

Consumers may eventually feel the impact through transport, freight and retail prices if the pressure persists. Exporters can receive more shillings when converting dollar earnings, but that benefit does not automatically offset higher domestic costs.

Economists will be watching the oil market, foreign-exchange demand and the response of the Bank of Uganda. Businesses are being encouraged to price cautiously while the market remains volatile.

SPORTS • UGANDA PREMIER LEAGUE

UPL title race enters new territory as early leaders challenge the traditional order

The opening rounds of the Uganda Premier League have produced an unusually competitive picture, with early leaders putting pressure on established clubs.

Bul have made one of the strongest starts, collecting maximum points from their opening fixtures and showing that the title conversation may not be limited to the usual favourites.

Blacks Power are also among the teams attracting attention, while Vipers have endured a difficult beginning and are still looking for their first points after fixtures were disrupted by continental commitments.

KCCA face another important test at Lugogo as they attempt to recover momentum. The early table should still be treated carefully because a long league season can change quickly, but the first results have already created an intriguing competition.

For supporters across Eastern Uganda and the rest of the country, the message is clear: the new season is offering plenty of reasons to watch closely, with underdogs showing confidence and traditional powers facing pressure.

AFRICA • HEALTH

UN sees progress in DRC Ebola response but warns outbreak remains severe

International health officials say the Ebola response in eastern Democratic Republic of Congo is showing encouraging signs, but they caution that it is too early to declare the outbreak under control.

Daily case numbers have fallen from the highest levels recorded in August, and several affected health zones have gone weeks without reporting new cases. Those trends offer hope that response teams are beginning to slow transmission.

The situation remains serious in North Kivu, where new cases continue to be recorded. Security challenges and movement of people make contact tracing and health interventions more difficult in some areas.

The United Nations and World Health Organization continue to stress sustained surveillance, treatment, safe practices and community cooperation. A decline in cases over a short period is encouraging, but health experts say the response must continue for months before the outbreak can safely be considered fully contained.

The developments matter to the wider Great Lakes region because cross-border movement can complicate outbreak control. Uganda and neighbouring countries therefore have a strong interest in continued monitoring and preparedness.

GLOBAL • ENERGY

Oil prices jump as Saudi and Libyan disruptions raise fresh supply fears

Global crude prices climbed sharply as disruptions affecting Saudi Arabia and Libya increased fears of tighter oil supplies.

Brent crude moved above $108 a barrel while US crude also gained strongly. The increase came amid disruption to important energy infrastructure and interruptions at several Libyan oil fields.

For oil-importing countries, sustained high prices can increase the cost of fuel, transport and production. Uganda is particularly sensitive to this type of shock because petroleum products are largely imported and the local currency has also been under pressure.

Businesses may respond by reviewing transport budgets, freight costs and import prices. Households can also feel the effect if higher fuel costs are passed through to public transport, delivery charges and consumer goods.

Whether the price rise becomes a prolonged trend will depend on the duration of the disruptions and the wider security situation around major shipping and energy routes.

ENTERTAINMENT • MBALE

Joshua Baraka brings B’rakaland energy to Mbale

Ugandan musician Joshua Baraka brought his B’rakaland EA Tour to Bandali Lounge in Mbale, drawing a lively crowd for a night of music and celebration.

The Mbale stop formed part of Baraka's first major tour across Uganda. The singer has grown from an emerging name into one of Uganda's prominent young musical acts, with his 2023 breakthrough hit Nana helping expand his audience.

Fans began arriving before the performance, and the venue filled as anticipation grew. Baraka later took the stage and performed several of his popular songs, with the audience singing along and creating a strong live-show atmosphere.

The event also demonstrated Mbale's potential as an entertainment destination. Major concerts bring together venues, performers, hospitality businesses, transport operators, food vendors and other parts of the local economy.

Baraka's tour continues to other Ugandan cities before the planned Kampala finale. For Mbale, the performance added another major entertainment event to the city's growing calendar.
